Cardiac arrest and ACLS

STEMI Algorithm

From the 12-lead to reperfusion

Reviewed by Alex G, BSN, RN, CEN, CCRN, CMC, NREMT-P · From the Reference tab of the app

Each step lists what to do, then the choices that lead on. In the app the algorithm is tapped through one decision at a time.

1 12-lead within 10 minutes of arrival

  • ST elevation in two contiguous leads (1 mm, or 2 mm in V2–V3 by age and sex), or new LBBB with ischemic features or a posterior MI pattern
  • Repeat every 15–30 minutes if the first is not diagnostic and symptoms continue

STEMI → step 2Inferior STEMI → step 3

2 First medications

  • Aspirin 162–325 mg chewed
  • P2Y12 inhibitor per cath lab protocol
  • Heparin bolus per protocol
  • Nitroglycerin for pain if SBP over 90 and no RV infarct or PDE-5 use; oxygen only if SpO₂ under 90%

PCI-capable hospital → step 4No PCI within 120 minutes → step 5

3 Inferior MI: check the right ventricle

  • Right-sided leads: ST elevation in V4R
  • RV infarct: give fluids for hypotension; avoid nitrates, diuretics and morphine

Continue → step 2

4 Primary PCI

  • Door-to-balloon 90 minutes or less (first medical contact to device 90 minutes, or 120 with transfer)
  • Cardiogenic shock: emergent revascularization; consider a microaxial pump in selected patients

End of this path.

5 Fibrinolysis

  • Within 12 hours of symptoms, door-to-needle 30 minutes or less
  • Then transfer for angiography: urgently if reperfusion fails (under 50% ST resolution at 60–90 minutes)
Safety Absolute contraindications include any prior intracranial hemorrhage, ischemic stroke within 3 months, active bleeding, suspected aortic dissection.

End of this path.

Drugs in this algorithm

  • Morphine

  • Shock Types at a GlanceCardiogenic shock: LV, RV, SCAI and MCS escalation: RV shock follows RV infarct (inferior STEMI, ST elevation in V4R), PE, ARDS, pulmonary hypertension and LVAD implantation. The dilated RV pushes the septum into the LV, so LV filling falls even with normal LV muscle. Give fluid until CVP is about 10–15 mm Hg, then stop; keep AV synchrony (pace heart block); avoid hypoxemia, hypercapnia, acidosis
