First five minutes

New Hypoxemia Algorithm

Probe, position and oxygen first; then DOPE or the bedside clues

Reviewed by Alex G, BSN, RN, CEN, CCRN, CMC, NREMT-P · From the Reference tab of the app

Each step lists what to do, then the choices that lead on. In the app the algorithm is tapped through one decision at a time.

1 Look at the patient: is the low SpO₂ real?

  • Not breathing or no pulse: call a code, open the airway, bag-mask with 100% O₂; CPR if pulseless
  • Look at color, work of breathing, RR and mental status; distressed or cyanotic: raise the O₂ now
  • Check the probe: clear pleth wave, warm finger, probe seated; try an ear or forehead probe
  • Real or unsure: call the respiratory therapist and the provider now; rapid response team outside the ICU

Poor waveform, patient comfortable → step 2Real, not intubated → step 3Real, on a ventilator (tube or tracheostomy) → step 4

2 Probe problem: fix it and recheck

  • Warm the hand, reseat the probe, limit motion, remove dark nail polish; ear or forehead probe for poor perfusion
  • Cold, vasoconstricted or on high-dose pressors: a weak signal can still hide real hypoxemia
  • Still unsure: ABG with co-oximetry; methylene blue dye and methemoglobin distort SpO₂
  • Recheck with a good waveform; tell the provider if it stays low

End of this path.

3 Sit up and raise the oxygen

  • Sit upright, head of bed 45° or more, unless hypotensive or on spine precautions
  • Raise O₂ to SpO₂ 92–96% (88–92% if at risk of CO₂ retention): nasal cannula up to 6 L/min, then nonrebreather 15 L/min
  • Listen to both sides; count the RR; look for secretions, wheeze, crackles, swelling and the fluid balance
  • Ask for an ABG and a portable chest x-ray

Next: treat what you find → step 5

4 On the ventilator: 100% O₂, then DOPE

  • FiO₂ to 100%; look at chest rise, exhaled Vt, peak pressure and the EtCO₂ waveform
  • Distressed or still falling: disconnect and bag with 100% O₂ and a PEEP valve; feel how easy it is
  • Displacement: tube depth at the teeth, EtCO₂ waveform. Obstruction: kinks, biting, secretions; pass a suction catheter
  • Pneumothorax: one side silent, high peak and plateau, falling BP. Equipment: circuit, O₂ supply, settings

Tube out, no EtCO₂ waveform, or catheter will not pass → step 6Signs of tension pneumothorax → step 7Better when bagged: equipment or settings → step 8DOPE clear: the lungs are worse → step 9

5 Not intubated: treat what you find

  • Secretions, weak cough or aspiration: stop tube feeds, suction (nasotracheal if needed), cough and chest physiotherapy with RT
  • Wheeze: albuterol 2.5–5 mg nebulized, plus ipratropium 0.5 mg in asthma or COPD
  • Crackles, JVD or overload: furosemide IV per order (40–80 mg, more if on a home dose); early NIV; nitroglycerin if SBP is high
  • Slow, shallow breathing and pinpoint pupils: naloxone 0.04–0.4 mg IV, titrated to breathing

One side silent with falling BP: tension pneumothorax → step 7Still under target on 15 L/min, or working hard → step 10Improving → step 11

6 Tube out or blocked

  • Tube out or no EtCO₂ waveform: deflate the cuff, remove the tube, bag-mask with 100% O₂ (oral airway, two hands)
  • Catheter will not pass: biting (bite block, sedation) or a plug; still blocked: remove the tube and bag-mask
  • Call anesthesia or the airway team to reintubate; airway cart, suction and RSI drugs to the bedside
  • Tracheostomy: remove a speaking valve or cap and the inner cannula, then suction; still blocked: deflate the cuff, then remove the tube
  • Tracheostomy out: O₂ to face and stoma; follow the Tracheostomy Emergency algorithm
Safety No EtCO₂ waveform means the tube is not in the trachea until proven otherwise, even in cardiac arrest.

End of this path.

7 Tension pneumothorax: decompress now

  • Clues: one side silent, falling SpO₂ and BP, high peak and plateau on the ventilator; tracheal shift is late
  • The provider decompresses now: needle or finger at the 4th–5th intercostal space, just anterior to the mid-axillary line
  • Do not wait for an x-ray; 100% O₂; chest tube tray to the bedside
  • A chest tube follows, then an x-ray and a blood gas

End of this path.

8 Better off the ventilator: equipment or settings

  • Keep bagging with 100% O₂ and PEEP while RT checks or replaces the circuit or ventilator
  • Look for leaks, disconnects, water in the circuit, the O₂ supply, and a wrong FiO₂, PEEP or Vt
  • Fighting the ventilator: treat pain and agitation; adjust flow, trigger or mode with RT

End of this path.

9 DOPE clear: lung or circulation problem

  • After suctioning or a disconnect: lung units may have collapsed; ask RT about re-recruiting and more PEEP
  • Sudden one-sided collapse: mucus plug; suction, chest physiotherapy, bronchoscopy
  • Crackles and a positive balance: diuresis per order. High peak, normal plateau: suction, bite block or bronchodilator
  • Falling EtCO₂ with hypotension: think PE or low cardiac output. Severe ARDS (P/F under 150): proning

Next: ABG and imaging → step 11

10 Escalate: high-flow or NIV

  • High-flow nasal cannula 40–60 L/min, FiO₂ to the SpO₂ target: first choice for most hypoxemic failure
  • NIV (CPAP or BiPAP) instead for cardiogenic pulmonary edema, or COPD with pH 7.35 or less and a high PaCO₂
  • BiPAP start: EPAP 5, IPAP 10–15 cm H₂O; only if awake, protecting the airway and not vomiting
  • Reassess within 1 hour: SpO₂, RR, effort; ABG at 1–2 h on NIV; ROX (SpO₂ ÷ FiO₂ ÷ RR) under 3.85 at 12 h predicts HFNC failure
Safety HFNC and NIV must not delay a needed intubation: agree on a failure trigger with the provider now.

Improving → step 11Failing or tiring → step 12

11 Find the cause: ABG and imaging

  • ABG: PaO₂ and P/F ratio; PaCO₂ and pH for hypoventilation or CO₂ retention; lactate
  • Portable chest x-ray; bedside ultrasound for lung sliding, B-lines, effusion and heart function
  • 12-lead ECG; sudden hypoxemia, tachycardia and clear lungs: ask about PE (CT angiography when safe to travel)
  • Common causes: edema, pneumonia or aspiration, plug or collapse, bronchospasm, pneumothorax, PE, ARDS, oversedation

Next: report → step 13

12 When to intubate

  • SpO₂ under 88–90% on maximal HFNC or NIV, or RR over 35 with exhaustion or paradoxical breathing
  • GCS 8 or less, cannot protect the airway or clear secretions, vomiting, or NIV not tolerated
  • Hypercapnia worsening on NIV: pH still under 7.25 after 1–2 hours, or falling; shock
  • Call anesthesia or the airway team; RSI checklist; preoxygenate on NIV or HFNC; push-dose pressor drawn up
Safety Expect hypotension and desaturation at induction: have fluid or a pressor ready and the most experienced operator.

End of this path.

13 Report (SBAR) and keep watching

  • S: SpO₂ on what support (device and L/min, or FiO₂ and PEEP), RR, HR, BP, when it started
  • B: diagnosis, recent procedures, fluid balance, opioids or sedatives, last chest x-ray, code status
  • A: work of breathing, mental status, breath sounds, ABG (pH, PaCO₂, PaO₂), P/F, the cause you suspect
  • R: bedside review; orders for imaging, ABG, HFNC or NIV, diuretic or nebulizer; ICU or airway team if failing
  • Recheck SpO₂, RR and work of breathing every 15 minutes until stable

End of this path.

Drugs in this algorithm

  • Furosemide
  • Albuterol
