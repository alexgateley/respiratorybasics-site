Patient Data

A patient with COPD has a post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC of 0.52 and an FEV₁ of 38% of predicted. What is the spirometric grade of airflow obstruction?

  1. GOLD 1, mild
  2. GOLD 4, very severe
  3. GOLD 2, moderate
  4. GOLD 3, severe

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: D

  • A. Grade 1 is an FEV₁ of 80% or more.
  • B. Grade 4 is below 30%.
  • C. Grade 2 is 50% to 79%.
  • D. Correct. With a ratio under 0.70, an FEV₁ of 30% to 49% of predicted is grade 3.

COPD is confirmed by a post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC below 0.70. Airflow obstruction is graded 1 to 4 by the FEV₁, and treatment is guided separately by symptoms and exacerbation history (groups A, B and E).

Initiation and Modification of Interventions

A patient with acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema is sitting upright with a respiratory rate of 34, an SpO₂ of 85% on a non-rebreather mask and a blood pressure of 176/98. Which respiratory support is indicated?

  1. CPAP 8 to 10 cm H₂O with oxygen
  2. Immediate intubation
  3. Nasal cannula at 6 L/min with the head of the bed raised
  4. Incentive spirometry

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: A

  • A. Correct. CPAP raises functional residual capacity, reduces preload and afterload, and lowers work of breathing in cardiogenic edema, reducing intubation rates; bilevel support is an alternative when hypercapnia is present.
  • B. Positive pressure by mask often avoids intubation in cardiogenic edema.
  • C. The patient is failing on a higher-concentration device.
  • D. Lung expansion exercises do not treat acute edema.

CPAP applications: obstructive sleep apnea, cardiogenic pulmonary edema, post-extubation atelectasis, and neonatal respiratory distress. Pressures are titrated to oxygenation and tolerance; monitoring includes blood pressure (positive pressure lowers preload), gastric distention, skin, and mental status. Hypotensive cardiogenic shock is treated differently.

Clinical Judgment: Pediatric and Neonatal

A 3-year-old with moderate croup has an SpO₂ of 91%. He cries and pulls at a simple mask, and his stridor gets louder each time the mask touches his face. Which oxygen delivery method should the therapist use?

  1. Nasal cannula at 4 L/min taped to both cheeks
  2. Nonrebreather mask held firmly in place with the head strap
  3. Blow-by oxygen while he sits on a parent's lap
  4. Oxygen tent with cool mist in his crib on the ward

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. A cannula seems less confining than a mask, but placing and taping the prongs also provokes crying. Firm restraint to keep them in place adds to the agitation that worsens his airway.
  • B. Tempting because it delivers the highest FiO₂, but strapping a mask on a toddler who is fighting it makes him cry harder and louder stridor follows. He already worsens every time a mask touches his face.
  • C. Correct. Agitation worsens croup, so less invasive techniques are preferred. StatPearls notes that blow-by oxygen may work better than a mask or cannula in these children, and a parent's lap keeps him calm.
  • D. Mist was used for croup in the past, but humidified air has not improved croup scores or admission rates. Placing a frightened toddler alone in a tent away from his parents also increases agitation.

In croup, crying and struggling increase inspiratory effort and turbulent flow through the swollen subglottis, which makes stridor and distress worse. Care is therefore arranged to keep the child calm: treatment on a parent's lap, oral rather than injected dexamethasone when possible, and no blood draws unless needed. Every child with a low saturation should receive oxygen, given in the least upsetting way. Hypoxemia or cyanosis is a sign of more severe croup and calls for close observation.

Clinical Judgment: Adult Patients

A COPD patient on noninvasive ventilation becomes unresponsive with a pH of 7.19 and a PaCO₂ of 92 mm Hg. He is intubated. Which initial ventilator strategy is appropriate?

  1. VT 6 to 8, rate 10 to 12, high flow, permissive hypercapnia
  2. Pressure control with inverse ratio ventilation
  3. Rate of 24 to normalize the PaCO₂ within the hour
  4. Volume control, tidal volume 8 mL/kg, rate 20, inspiratory time 1.2 seconds

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: A

  • A. Correct. Obstructive disease needs a long expiratory time to prevent auto-PEEP; the PaCO₂ is lowered gradually toward the patient’s chronic baseline rather than to normal, which avoids post-hypercapnic alkalosis.
  • B. Inverse ratio shortens expiration and worsens trapping.
  • C. Rapid normalization causes alkalosis and hemodynamic compromise, and the rate causes trapping.
  • D. A high rate and long inspiration cause breath stacking in obstructed lungs.

Invasive ventilation in chronic lung disease: settings that avoid dynamic hyperinflation, monitoring of auto-PEEP and plateau pressure, extrinsic PEEP to offset auto-PEEP during spontaneous breathing, sedation strategies that preserve drive, early consideration of weaning to noninvasive ventilation, and attention to nutrition and weakness that prolong ventilation.

The exams

Respiratory Basics covers NBRC RTE, AHA ACLS. Switch exams any time; mock exams follow each exam's length, time limit and topic proportions.

NBRC

Respiratory Therapy Exam (RTE)

1,000 practice questions · 160-question mock exams, 4 h limit

The 2027 Respiratory Therapy Examination has 185 questions (160 scored and 25 unscored pretest questions) in a single 4-hour sitting, with a lower cut score for the CRT credential and a higher one for the RRT. Topic proportions follow the NBRC detailed content outline effective January 2027.

ACLS

550+ practice questions · 50-question mock exams, 1 h limit

The AHA ACLS provider written exam has 50 questions. AHA does not publish a topic breakdown, so each mock draws questions at random from all adult ACLS topics.
